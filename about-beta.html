Our story

From a conversation to a magazine.

01

The spark

The Catalyst began as a conversation between a few students who felt that STEM research, happening all around them in one of the most scientifically active cities in the country, wasn’t reaching the people who should care most about it. Washington is home to world-class universities, federal research agencies and a policy world where science matters. We wanted a publication that reflected that.

02

The build

We started small: a handful of writers, no budget and a lot of shared Google Docs. Contributors from across the D.C. area joined, students from GW, Georgetown, Howard, Johns Hopkins and beyond, each bringing a different lens to the same goal. The magazine grew with the people in it more than from any master plan.

03

Today

The Catalyst is now a cross-institutional student magazine with a growing readership and a clear editorial identity. We’re still student-run, still independent and still driven by the same conviction: good science writing can change how people think. We’re glad you’re here.

Our commitment

The standards behind every piece.

What we promise readers, the researchers we write about, and each other.

  1. 01

    Editorial rigor

    Every article is fact-checked against primary sources before it reaches readers. Our editors review for accuracy, clarity and fairness, and we correct promptly when we get something wrong.

  2. 02

    Accessible writing

    We write for curious readers, not specialists. The goal is to make complex research understandable without dumbing it down, respecting both the science and the audience.

  3. 03

    Independence

    The Catalyst is editorially independent. Our coverage is guided by journalistic judgment, not institutional affiliation or outside influence.
